# GameChangers / GC Education — Design System Notes

## 1. Design Style Summary

The overall design style can be described as:

**Gen-Z EdTech + startup marketing + editorial social media.**

It is not a traditional education-consulting aesthetic built around dark blue, gold, serif typography, formal photography, and conservative layouts.

Instead, the brand feels closer to a **youth startup, creator brand, or ambitious student community**.

The visual system is built around:

- Bold communication
- Strong typography
- Youthful energy
- Student-focused imagery
- Achievement-driven content
- Clear visual hierarchy
- Modular layouts
- Social-first compositions
- International university references
- Startup-style branding

A concise design definition:

> **Bold Gen-Z educational editorial design with startup aesthetics, oversized grotesk typography, strong visual hierarchy, student cutout photography, achievement-focused numerical graphics, modular cards, energetic social-media compositions, and international-university visual references.**

## 3. Core Visual Philosophy

The design system follows a simple principle:

### One strong idea per visual

Every post, slide, section, or card should have an immediately understandable focal point.

Typical hierarchy:

1. Strong headline
2. Visual focal point
3. Proof or result
4. Supporting information
5. Call to action

The user should understand the core message within approximately one second.

The design prioritizes:

**clarity + aspiration + energy**

over decorative complexity.

---

## 4. Typography

### General Style

Typography is one of the most important parts of the identity.

The style uses:

- Modern sans-serif fonts
- Grotesk typography
- Geometric typography
- Heavy weights
- Large headlines
- Tight hierarchy
- Short text blocks

Suitable font directions:

- Manrope
- Inter
- Montserrat
- TT Commons
- Gilroy
- Helvetica Now
- Neue Haas Grotesk
- Söhne
- Graphik-style grotesks

### Headline Style

Headlines should be:

- Large
- Bold
- Short
- Direct
- High contrast
- Visually dominant

Example:

```text
ПОСТУПИЛ В
TOP-20 UNIVERSITY
```

or:

```text
3 ОШИБКИ
ИЗ-ЗА КОТОРЫХ
ТЕБЕ ОТКАЖУТ
```

### Supporting Typography

Secondary text should be:

- Smaller
- Simple
- Clean
- Easy to scan
- Used sparingly

Do not create long paragraphs inside visual content.

### Numerical Typography

Large numbers are especially important.

Examples:

```text
$50,000
SCHOLARSHIP
```

```text
TOP 20
UNIVERSITY
```

```text
5 000 000 ₸
FUNDING
```

Numbers should often become graphic elements themselves.

---

## 5. Typography Hierarchy

Recommended hierarchy:

### H1
Primary message.

- Very large
- Bold or extra-bold
- 1–3 lines maximum

### H2
Context or secondary statement.

- Medium-large
- Semi-bold

### Metric
Scholarship, ranking, funding, acceptance result, number of projects, etc.

- Extremely visible
- Often larger than normal body text
- Can use a different scale from the headline

### Body
Supporting explanation.

- Short
- Minimal
- High legibility

### Metadata
University name, category, date, label, program name.

- Small
- Low visual priority

## 6. Layout Style

The layout system is typically:

- Modular
- Card-based
- Editorial
- Asymmetrical when useful
- Built around strong hierarchy
- Spacious enough to avoid clutter
- Optimized for mobile viewing

### Common Composition

```text
[small logo / brand label]

BIG HEADLINE
BIG HEADLINE

[student image / cutout]

$68,000
SCHOLARSHIP

[university / program label]
```

Alternative educational layout:

```text
3 ОШИБКИ
ИЗ-ЗА КОТОРЫХ
ТЕБЕ ОТКАЖУТ

01
Weak extracurriculars

02
Generic essay

03
No application strategy
```

### Grid Behavior

Use:

- Strong vertical alignment
- Large text blocks
- Distinct content zones
- Clear grouping
- Minimal decorative fragmentation

Avoid:

- Tiny text everywhere
- Dense dashboards
- Overcomplicated grids
- Too many visual focal points
- Excessive decorative shapes

## 8. Imagery

### Main Image Style

Use:

- Real students
- Mentors
- Founders
- Teams
- Events
- University environments
- Project presentations
- Competitions
- Hackathons

Student imagery should feel:

- Authentic
- Aspirational
- Energetic
- International
- Young
- Confident

### Cutout Photography

A strong recurring visual technique is:

**subject cutouts placed over graphic backgrounds.**

This helps create:

- More dynamic layouts
- Better control over composition
- Stronger social-media aesthetic
- Clear focal points

### Avoid

- Generic corporate stock photos
- Fake boardroom scenes
- Empty campus photography without context
- Overly staged business imagery
- Traditional graduation clichés unless needed

The visual system should frequently highlight measurable outcomes.

Examples:

- Scholarship amount
- University ranking
- Number of applications
- Competition result
- Funding raised
- Project users
- Acceptance rate
- Number of students
- Number of countries
- Scores
- Awards

The graphic language should communicate:

> **proof, not promises.**

This is a core part of the brand style.

## 10. Content Hierarchy

A typical communication structure:

### Ambition
What does the student want?

### Action
What did they build, study, enter, or improve?

### Achievement
What measurable result happened?

This can be summarized as:

**Ambition → Action → Achievement**

Design should reinforce this sequence.

Recommended card style:

## 12. Shape Language

Recommended shapes:

- Rectangles
- Rounded cards
- Simple pills
- Large blocks
- Minimal circles
- Highlight labels

Avoid overly decorative:

- Waves
- Abstract education illustrations
- Random gradients
- Excessive blobs
- Corporate infographic shapes

Decorative elements should support hierarchy, not compete with it.

---

## 13. Color Direction

The strongest characteristic is not a specific color palette, but the use of:

- High contrast
- Strong accent colors
- Clear background/text separation
- Youthful combinations
- Digital-first colors

Recommended strategy:

### Base
Neutral background:

- White
- Off-white
- Black
- Dark charcoal

### Accent
Use one strong brand accent.

Possible directions:

- Electric blue
- Bright green
- Lime
- Orange
- Red
- Purple

### Principle

Do not use many accents simultaneously.

Prefer:

**neutral base + one powerful accent**

for a cleaner, more premium execution.

---

## 14. Visual Contrast

Contrast should come from:

- Large vs small typography
- Dark vs light
- Neutral vs accent
- Image vs flat graphic
- Large number vs supporting text
- Bold headline vs minimal body

The design should not depend on heavy shadows or excessive effects.

---

## 15. Motion Style

For reels, stories, landing pages, and interactive content:

Recommended motion:

- Fast text reveals
- Number count-ups
- Slide-in cards
- Masked image reveals
- Scale transitions
- Simple parallax
- Short kinetic typography
- Smooth but energetic transitions

Motion should feel:

**fast, confident, modern**

rather than cinematic or luxurious.

---

## 16. University Visual References

International university names and identities are useful visual credibility markers.

Common visual ingredients can include:

- University logos
- Campus names
- Rankings
- Country labels
- Scholarship numbers
- Acceptance statistics
- Flags used carefully
- Application outcomes

These should support the student achievement rather than dominate the entire design.

## 17. Tone of Voice

Copy should feel:

- Direct
- Youthful
- Clear
- Motivating
- Result-focused
- Conversational
- Confident

Use:

- Short sentences
- Numbers
- Questions
- Clear claims
- Action verbs
- Specific outcomes

Examples:

```text
Как поступить в TOP-20?
```

```text
3 ошибки, которые убивают твою заявку
```

```text
$70,000 scholarship.
Вот как мы это сделали.
```

```text
Не просто проект.
Проект, который усиливает заявку.
```

Avoid overly formal writing like:

> Our organization provides comprehensive consulting services for students interested in international higher education opportunities.

Prefer:

> Хочешь в сильный университет?  
> Начни строить профиль сейчас.

The design works because it matches the product.

The product is about:

- Mentorship
- University admissions
- Student development
- Projects
- Competitions
- Hackathons
- Startups
- Scholarships
- International opportunities

Therefore the design is built around:

- Momentum
- Achievement

> **We help students do things, not just study theory.**

## 20. What NOT to Copy

Do not blindly imitate every social-media trend.

Avoid:

- Excessive gradients
- Too many stickers
- Too many emojis inside the design
- Random visual effects
- Overcrowded cards
- Trendy typography that reduces readability
- Extremely loud colors everywhere
- Too many fonts
- Generic motivational quotes

The strongest parts to borrow are:

1. Content hierarchy
2. Achievement-driven storytelling
3. Big typography
4. Youthful energy
5. Real student imagery
6. Numerical proof
7. Short communication
8. Modular layouts

---

## 21. Recommended Upgraded Direction

A stronger version of the same style would be:

**GameChangers energy + Linear-level cleanliness + editorial typography.**

Keep:

- Youth
- Ambition
- Strong results
- Student imagery
- Big headlines
- Social-first communication

Improve:

- Spacing
- Grid consistency
- Typography
- Color discipline
- Image treatment
- UI consistency
- Motion
- Brand coherence

The upgraded result should feel:

**20–30% more premium and disciplined without becoming corporate.**
